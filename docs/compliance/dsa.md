# Digital Services Act: category and duties (draft)

A draft for legal review, not legal advice ([README](README.md)). Source: [Regulation (EU) 2022/2065](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065) (DSA). Our own inferences are marked as such.

## 1. Which category is AnyPlay?

The DSA has nested categories of intermediary service. The definitions are in [Art. 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065):

- **Intermediary service, Art. 3(g):** mere conduit, caching or hosting.
- **Hosting, Art. 3(g)(iii):** "the storage of information provided by, and at the request of, a recipient of the service".
- **Online platform, Art. 3(i):** "a hosting service that, at the request of a recipient of the service, stores and disseminates information to the public". A minor, purely ancillary feature of another service is excluded.
- **Dissemination to the public, Art. 3(k):** "making information available, at the request of the recipient of the service who provided the information, to a potentially unlimited number of third parties".

### Our proposal: a hosting service today, an online platform once something is public

**Today (inference from the code at this commit).**

- The cloud stores information that players provide at their own request: handle, device name, reports with free text, game titles and opaque session data. That is hosting.
- Nothing is made available to "a potentially unlimited number of third parties". Presence and friend lists reach friends. Session visibility is `friends`, `invite` or `closed` (network `spec/cloud-api.md`). The only open lookup is of a handle or ID by a signed-in account, and it returns the handle only. So the cloud is **not** an online platform yet. (The lawyer should check the lookup: any signed-in player with a handle can find any other, and sign-up is open.)
- It is **not** merely a conduit: it stores the information beyond transmission.
- The relay carries encrypted traffic and cannot read it. For the relay alone, mere conduit under Art. 3(g)(i) is the closest fit (inference). The operator should treat the relay and the cloud as one service for the contact points and terms below.

**Planned (network `docs/SOCIAL.md`).** A **web profile** page "showing only what the player made public", privacy settings that let a player open parts of a profile to "everyone", a feed and messages. A public web profile is a player's information made available to anyone with a browser. We think that moves the cloud into the online platform category (inference, Art. 3(i) and (k)). Private one-to-one messages and friends-only items would not be public. A group chat open to strangers might be, a question for the lawyer ([open-questions.md](open-questions.md)).

**Not a very large platform.** The extra duties for very large platforms start at 45 million monthly recipients (Art. 24(4) mentions the figure). A volunteer cloud is far below it.

### The micro and small enterprise rule

- [Art. 15(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065): the transparency report duty "shall not apply" to micro or small enterprises that are not very large platforms.
- [Art. 19(1)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065): Section 3 (Art. 20 to 28: complaints, out-of-court settlement, trusted flaggers, misuse, interface design, recommender systems and minors) does not apply to online platforms that are micro or small enterprises, with one exception, Art. 24(3). The exclusion lasts 12 months after the enterprise loses that status.
- The definition is in [Recommendation 2003/361/EC, Annex Art. 2](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32003H0361): a small enterprise has fewer than 50 persons and a yearly turnover or balance sheet of at most EUR 10 million; a micro enterprise fewer than 10 persons and at most EUR 2 million. Linked and partner enterprises are added up (Annex Art. 3 and 6).
- **An open question:** the Recommendation speaks of an "enterprise", which is any entity doing an economic activity. Whether an unincorporated volunteer project is an enterprise at all, and what happens to the status when it forms an association or company, is for the lawyer. We assume it counts as micro (inference).
- Art. 28 (protection of minors) sits in Section 3. For a micro or small platform it is therefore not binding as such. We follow its aims anyway ([network ADR 0012](https://github.com/anyplay-gg/network/blob/main/docs/adr/0012-private-by-default.md) cites it) because the GDPR asks for data protection by default ([Art. 25(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679)) and Art. 28(1) is the stated benchmark if the exclusion is lost.

### What does not apply

Art. 22 (trusted flaggers), 25 (interface design), 26 (advertising), 27 (recommender systems) and 30 to 32 (marketplaces) are Section 3 or 4 duties of online platforms or marketplaces. The first four are excluded for micro and small platforms by Art. 19(1); Art. 30 to 32 are excluded by Art. 29(1) for micro and small platforms that allow consumers to conclude contracts with traders, and the cloud has no marketplace. AnyPlay has no ads and no recommender today.

### The date

The DSA applies from 17 February 2024 ([Art. 93(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065)). We could not retrieve Art. 93 itself from EUR-Lex in the tool we used; our source for the date is the research report and a General Court order quoting Art. 93(2) (EUR-Lex 62023CO0639(01)). Check it against the Official Journal PDF before relying on it.

## 3. What exists in the cloud today

| Piece | In code | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Report an account | `POST /v1/reports`, `reports` table, 5 reasons, 1,000 characters, 20 per day per reporter | Signed-in players only. No content reference. No status. |
| Look at reports | `GET /admin/v1/reports`, `anyplay-cloud admin reports [--limit N]` | One shared admin token. Shows reporter, reported, reason, details. |
| Ban | `POST /admin/v1/bans`, `anyplay-cloud admin ban <handle> [--days N] [--reason TEXT]` | Signs the account out at once, removes it from sessions, blocks the e-mail hash. |
| Unban | `DELETE /admin/v1/bans/{handle}` | Deletes the ban row. |
| Block | `PUT /v1/blocks/{id}` | Private; also ends a friendship, requests and invitations. |
| Log of actions | `tracing::warn!("account banned")` with account ID and end date | Not a record; no actor; goes to the ordinary log with whatever retention that has. |

## 4. Engineering tasks

The code is not changed in this pull request. These are proposed tasks, in order.

| ID | Task | Closes | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **A notice form** for illegal content, open to people without an account: a public web page and `POST /v1/notices` with reasons, exact location (account handle or ID, later a content ID), the notifier's name and e-mail, and a good-faith checkbox ([Art. 16(1), (2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065)). Rate-limit by IP in memory as elsewhere. Link it from the launcher and the terms. | Art. 16 | Keep the in-app report (`reports`) for rule breaks; a notice for illegality is a different reason (`illegal`). | T2 | **A receipt** mail with a reference number on a notice, and a **decision mail** with the outcome and redress options ([Art. 16(4), (5)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32022R2065)).
